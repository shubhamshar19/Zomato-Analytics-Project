As an aspiring data analyst, I applied my SQL and Power BI skills to analyze Zomato’s restaurant, menu, and order data, uncovering key insights into cuisine performance, pricing trends, customer ordering behavior, and restaurant contributions.

-- Business Problem --

The business problem centers on understanding how restaurants, menus, and customers influence overall sales and profitability, with the goal of helping a food delivery platform make smarter decisions. The company needs to know which cuisines and menu items bring in the most revenue and profit, how pricing affects customer demand, and whether customers tend to be loyal or mostly one-time users.

 It’s also important to assess performance at both the restaurant and city levels to identify top performers and regional growth opportunities. Spotting anomalies, like invalid orders, ensures more accurate reporting and analysis. By combining SQL-driven insights with an interactive dashboard, this project highlights key profit drivers, customer retention patterns, and market opportunities that can inform strategies around pricing, marketing, and expansion.

--Business Questions For Analysis--

1. How many total orders were placed?

2. What is the total revenue across all orders?

3. What is the average revenue per order?   

4. Which cities have the highest order counts?

5. Which cuisines contribute most revenue?

6. Which cuisines do males vs females prefer?

7. What percentage of orders are veg vs non-veg?

8. Which age groups spend the most?

9. Which restaurants generate the most revenue? (Top 10 Restaurants)

10. How does order volume change with price — which items are overpriced or underpriced?



-- Analysis Report --


Zomato’s data shows how customer demand, restaurant performance, and regional growth shape the food delivery market. With 150K orders, 100K active customers, and revenue crossing 206M, the platform has built strong traction across cities like Bangalore, Delhi, Pune, and Mumbai. Popular cuisines such as North Indian–Chinese (31%), Indian–Chinese (26%), and North Indian (16%) emerge as the biggest revenue drivers, reflecting customer preference for comfort foods. Revenue trends also highlight a peak in 2018–2019, signaling a phase of strong growth.

Customer behavior adds another layer of insight. The average spend per customer is 18.5K, with people in their 20s and early 30s spending the most. Gender patterns reveal that males order more Chinese and Indian food, while females lean toward North Indian cuisines. Food choices are also clear—non-vegetarian dishes account for 78% of orders, while vegetarian food makes up 21%, pointing to a strong tilt in demand.

On the restaurant side, Zomato hosts 1,436 menu items, with an average price of 196.91 per dish. Outlets like Agrawal Sweets lead in revenue, and the top cuisines contribute the bulk of sales. Pricing trends reveal customer sensitivity to high menu rates, while city-level analysis highlights Bikaner, Bangalore, and Delhi as hotspots. This project helped me use SQL and Power BI to turn raw transactional data into meaningful insights, uncovering profit drivers, customer patterns, and growth opportunities for the food delivery industry.
